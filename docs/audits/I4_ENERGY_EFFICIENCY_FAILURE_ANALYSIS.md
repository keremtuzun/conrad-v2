# I4 energy-efficiency failure analysis

Status: current-code development diagnosis complete for the V4 incumbent; candidate ablations in progress. The V4 formal verdict remains **FAIL, 4/5**. Its random-view information-per-kJ benefit was +0.037653 with paired 95% CI [-0.000208, +0.074893]. The 8002200-8002229 final worlds are historical evidence only and will not select or calibrate this repair. The unread 8002230-8002259 and 8002300-8002319 worlds remain closed.

## Evidence and boundary

The tracked E007 development (8002000-8002039) and validation (8002100-8002139) records contain mission totals and view outcome counts. They do **not** contain per-view energy, predicted cost, realized arrival time, or a time series of energy. The old Unity final records also retain mission totals; its local run bundles may support a descriptive audit, but cannot tune a new model. This cycle therefore instruments separate current-code development re-executions below. In particular, the archived `ABANDONED_MISSION_END` count alone cannot establish its energy cost or causal effect on the gate metric.

This mismatch has not yet been isolated to code drift, instrumentation, or another cause. The historical E007 rows cannot be used as current-code calibration data until reproducibility is resolved. They remain valid historical context; prospective calibration requires current-code development traces and a reproducibility check before validation.

### Current-code historical development trace, 40 worlds

The current-code Python surrogate re-executed all 40 historical development seeds for diagnosis only. It found 64 flown views and 16 `ABANDONED_MISSION_END` views. The latter consumed 31,802.5 J during their open-view intervals, 517.6 s of mission time summed across worlds, and 76.1 m of estimated motion. The 64 completed views exceeded the planner's time estimate by a mean 4.49 s (MAE 4.53 s; 93.75% underestimated), estimated distance by 2.50 m (MAE 2.56 m; 93.75% underestimated), and energy by 4,607.8 J (MAE 4,607.8 J; 100% underestimated). The energy estimate covers modeled navigation plus sensor dwell whereas the meter covers controller, propulsion and hotel load, so its systematic gap is partly a cost-definition mismatch. Abandoned views are right-censored and excluded from completed-view calibration errors. Full distributions and intervals are in `artifacts/experiments/I4-ENERGY-DIAG/development_summary_historical.json`. These re-executions diverge from the archived E007 mission outcomes and are not formal evidence.

The mission-end abandonment mechanism is more specific than ordinary path underprediction. In all 16 abandoned views, the goal began only 0.010-0.048 m from the estimated current pose, below the trajectory generator's 0.05 m arc step. `TrajectoryGenerator.generate` created a two-point path, set both endpoint speeds to zero, and divided twice the segment length by a fallback `1e-6` speed. The resulting *planned* trajectories lasted 20,917-95,077 s and held 104,586-475,387 points. The navigation stack requires trajectory time to elapse before completing its goal, even when the view ledger has recorded pose and aim tolerance for longer than the required dwell. For example, seed 8002001 was inside both tolerances for 26.1 s but its 52,135 s trajectory remained `EXECUTING` at mission end. This explains the observed abandoned-view status and wasted controller activity without referring to hidden Twin state. A narrow correction to short-leg trajectory timing is now an explicit development candidate; a horizon filter based only on the old six-second prediction cannot repair this case.

### Independent new development baseline, 40 worlds

On 8100000-8100039, current-code V4 read the defect in 20/40 worlds. Mean hidden-state improvement was 0.388759, mean info/kJ 0.021475, and mean metered energy 16,736.6 J on the Python kernel. Of 70 accepted views, 59 were flown and 11 ended at mission clock, consuming 23,042.4 J over 363.1 summed view-seconds. Ten of those 11 had a 0.016-0.047 m initial leg and a generated trajectory of 32,785-93,412 s. The remaining abandoned view had a 0.102 m leg and 1.1 s trajectory, so it needs a separate explanation; the short-leg failure is dominant but not universal. Only one of the 11 abandoned views had `start + predicted total view time > 100 s`, confirming that the old time estimate did not warn the horizon filter.

The exact meter-delta phase totals across the 40 missions were 317.3 kJ nominal transit, 196.0 kJ inspection approach, 77.5 kJ inspection dwell, 74.3 kJ hold and 4.3 kJ revisit. These sum to 669.4 kJ, the 40-world metered total apart from the 2.5 J startup draw in each world. Propulsion-versus-base subcomponents are estimates from reported thruster current; the phase totals themselves reconcile to the battery meter. After the first direct target revision, the 22 worlds with such a revision consumed another 77.3 kJ in total (3.52 kJ mean). This is not automatically wasted energy because nominal transit and other mission duties may remain required; no early termination is inferred from it.

Across the 59 completed views, total view time was underestimated by 4.91 s on average (100% of views), estimated full-view motion by 2.70 m (98.3%), true full-view motion by 0.95 m (98.3%), and metered full-view energy by 4,017.2 J (100%). For the *approach only*, predicted distance exceeded truth-side approach motion by 0.10 m on average, so the full-view distance residual largely reflects station-keeping motion, not a general route-length underestimate. The mean approach time residual was +2.45 s and approach-energy residual +3,123.4 J. The exact bias, MAE, RMSE, quantiles and underprediction fractions, with abandoned views censored, are in `artifacts/experiments/I4-ENERGY-DIAG/development_summary_new_V4_full.json`.

The opt-in short-leg correction was then run on the same 40 development worlds. It converted most of the pathological goal executions into completed views: 81 flown and 3 mission-end abandoned views, compared with 59 and 11 for V4. Yet target read stayed 20/40, mean energy rose by 190.6 J, and info/kJ moved by only +0.000084. Therefore the trajectory defect is real, but fixing it alone does not produce material I4 energy-efficiency headroom. A two-offset calibration fitted on the first 20 corrected development worlds improves check-set completed-view time MAE from 4.32 to 2.27 s and energy MAE from 3,280.5 to 2,596.7 J; its mission-level effect is still pending ablation. This calibration measures the Python surrogate only and must not be assumed valid for Unity power scale without a Unity development check.

## Current cost path

`Deliberation.navigation_cost` supplies `PlanningRequest.navigation_cost`. It checks belief-side free space, then uses Euclidean distance, a 1.6 multiplier near surveyed structure, and an optional blocked-corridor multiplier. Time is that distance divided by `cruise_speed_mps`; energy is `ENERGY_PER_M_J` times distance. This is a heuristic cost, not the route length or propulsion demand returned by `NavigationStack`. `ExecutionFilter` adds sensor dwell and power and compares those amounts with remaining mission time and energy before ranking. V4 preserves the V3 Bayesian EIG ratio ranker. The navigation stack later executes a trajectory in a learned occupancy map, so its actual travel, time and energy can diverge from the heuristic. The direction and size of that error have not yet been measured.

`NavigationStack` already owns an `AStarPlanner` with the active belief-map occupancy query. Its `set_goal` uses that planner to generate the executable route and then a trajectory. Any route-length repair should expose a navigation estimate to MCBR through the existing `navigation_cost` callback; a separate A* inside MCBR would duplicate route policy. Repeated route solves per candidate have a potential planning-latency cost that development must measure.

### First two current-code development diagnostics

These are examples from current-code Python-kernel runs, insufficient to fit a model or select a candidate. Seed 8002000 used 20,900.6 J, of which 7,002.5 J occurred during inspection approach, 4,201.8 J during inspection dwell, 8,088.6 J during nominal transit and 1,605.2 J during hold. Its first direct target revision was at 49.3 s, followed by 6,212.3 J of mission energy. Its first flown view had a predicted 28.4 s and 568 J versus realized 28.0 s and approximately 8,561 J over the view interval. Seed 8002001 had a view predicted at 6.0 s and 31 J that remained in flight for 26.1 s until mission end, consuming approximately 1,581 J. Both runs use the unmodified V4 mechanism. The view interval includes the actual controller, station keeping and base load, whereas the planner's prospective cost is a simplified navigation-plus-sensor amount, so these numbers reveal a cost-definition mismatch as well as possible model bias. A population analysis must separate approach, dwell and base load before calibration.

## Energy-accounting inspection

The Python kernel accumulates propulsion power `thruster_power_w_per_n15 * sum(abs(thrust)**1.5)` plus hotel power once per physics step, into `energy_used_j`. Hardware power state exposes this accumulated value; the truth recorder writes it at mission end. The Unity `PowerModel` accumulates thruster electrical power plus separate compute, sensor and communications loads once per step; its mission tracker reads hardware power state at finish. Both arms in a Unity comparison use that same meter, but Python and Unity energy scales and components differ. The Python kernel does not meter a separate sensor load, although the planner charges sensor power in its prospective budget. No double count is apparent from these source paths; an observed end-to-end reconciliation is still required. The gate's `1000 * hidden_state_error_improvement / energy_j` formula remains unchanged.

## Diagnostic measurements required before mechanism choice

Instrument development runs without exposing truth to the planner. For each control interval record elapsed time, estimated and truth-side distance separately, total battery-energy delta, propulsion and hotel-energy deltas, executive/navigation phase, active view ID, and whether target evidence has already been acquired. For each accepted view join the planning cost and remaining horizon at acceptance to its ledger outcome, arrival, dwell, distance, energy, and information revision. Report bias, MAE, RMSE, quantiles, and underprediction rates for time, distance and energy, with incomplete views treated explicitly rather than assigned an invented arrival. Attribute energy to mission-end, blocked-route, no-information, redundant, off-pose and other abandonments without double counting overlapping phases. Post-satisfaction energy is diagnostic only; mission safety and semantics govern any transition change.

## Selection rule before new validation

A new candidate must retain closed-loop behavior, hidden-state reconstruction advantage over fixed, random and coverage views, truth separation, and safety. Among candidates meeting those conditions on new development worlds, compare information/kJ, no-value energy, mission-end abandonment, cost calibration and information/time. Choose one candidate before reading the new validation split. If validation does not show a material benefit with adequate headroom over random, stop without opening a final split. The ch25 source wording compares the full adaptive production system with simple-view systems under matched mission/time or energy budgets; this cycle will therefore use a **full production-stack comparison**, as the prior V4 protocol declared. Baseline execution differences and their interpretive limit must be explicit before validation.
